NEW IN 0.56.0 - Waypoint Mode: choose your ships' route
  - When you move ships, a crosshair button sits at the bottom right of
    the map, just above the game log (pointing at it says "Waypoint
    Mode"). Click it, or press X, to plot the route on the map yourself
    instead of letting the game choose it:
      1. If ships move from more than one system, first pick the system
         whose ships you are routing (highlighted).
      2. The systems the ships can step into next are highlighted: click
         them (or move the cursor and press A) one by one. A numbered line
         shows the route; a dashed red ring marks a gravity rift.
      3. Click the system you are moving into to finish (or press
         START). B takes the last stop back; X goes back to the automatic
         route.
    The move list comes back with the route listed under it. Ground

NEW IN 0.55.0 - the final bot pass
  - Stop the winner: every bot works out, from what anyone at the table
    can see (points, the public objectives each player meets right now,

    the winning score at the coming status phase. It then tries taking

INSTALL (OnionOS)
  Copy the "TwilightImperium" folder into the "App" folder on your SD card,
  replacing the old one. Open Apps > Twilight Imperium.

STARTING A GAME
  New game: choose 3-8 players, your faction (or random), your colour,
  10 or 14 victory points, the difficulty (Easy / Normal / Hard / Competitive), who plays
  your seat (you, or the computer while you watch), the opponents'
  personalities (A on "Personalities": each one Random or a chosen
  archetype) and how fast the computer players act. Your home system is at the bottom of the map (with
  3 or 4 players, where there's no single bottom spot: the lower right
  one); everyone else is the computer.
  The game saves itself constantly - choose "Continue game" on the title
  screen to pick up where you left off.

WATCHING THE COMPUTER PLAYERS
  Each thing a computer player does is shown for a moment before the next:
  - "Sol's turn" / "Sol ends turn" / "Sol passes" / "Round 3 ends" banners.
  - Lower right: the strategy card being taken or played (flashing), or a
    command token for a tactical action.
  - The activated system flashes in the player's colour - bright RED and
    blinking when enemy forces are there (a fight is coming). Arrows show
    ships moving in.
  - Combat panel on the right: both sides' units, every dice roll (green =
    hit) and who wins.
  - Top bar: whose turn it is and who plays next.
  - Action cards played by anyone appear in the lower right (red card).
  - Exploration cards (blue cultural, red hazardous, green industrial,
    purple frontier) and relics (gold) open in the middle of the map with
    their full text and wait until you press A. To have them show briefly
    instead (like action cards), change MENU > "Cards": wait for A /
    yours wait for A (only your own cards stop the game) / timed.
  - During the agenda phase the right-hand panel shows the agenda, what
    For / Against (or the election) does, and the votes as they come in.
  Hold A to fast-forward. Change the pace in MENU > AI speed
  (Slow / Normal / Fast / Instant).

CONTROLS
  D-pad        move the map cursor / move through a list
  A            choose / confirm. Hold A while the computer plays to speed up.
  B            back or clear - and when there is nothing left to back out
               of in the question, go back one step (undo, see below). B
               never answers a question: "No" / "Skip" are chosen with A
  START        confirm in editors (moving, producing, paying, hits, tokens)
  SELECT       players overview
  R2           the map view (R2 again to return); A there opens the tile
               under the cursor, a faction's sheet on its info tiles, or
               the cards on a play-area tile
  L2           side panel: system details or players
  Y            in editors: fill in a suggestion; on map choices: list view;
               otherwise: game log
  X            on map choices: jump to the next system you can pick;
               otherwise: objectives
  L1 / R1      zoom the map (in lists and cards: switch page / list)
  MENU         the faction web (Negotiations) and the game menu:
               negotiations, objectives, laws, your cards, faction sheet,
               units, technology, planets, players, game log, AI speed,
               card pop-ups, borders, info tiles, music and sound volume,
               save and quit

SOUND (0.27.0)
  Music and sound effects play through the Miyoo's speaker (the volume
  buttons work as usual). Options on the title screen and the game menu set
  the music and the effects separately (0 = off). If there is no sound at
  all: System info (Device tests) shows what the game found - please send
  launch_log.txt and ti4.log from the game's folder. If the game won't start
  since 0.27.0, put an empty file called "nosound" in its folder (the game
  then starts without the firmware's sound helper; it also does this by
  itself if it stops straight away). music.mp3 in the game's folder is the
  music - you can replace it with any MP3.

GOING BACK (B)
  B first backs out of the question in front of you: it clears what you
  set in an editor, skips an optional choice, answers "No", or returns from
  the list to the map. When there's nothing left to back out of, B takes
  back your last choice ("Went back") as long as nothing final has happened
  since: no dice were rolled, no hidden card was drawn or revealed, and no
  other player has acted. Example: you can go back from "move ships" to
  "activate which system?" and to "choose your action", but not after a
  combat has been rolled. (Before 0.19.0 this was on SELECT.)

ACTIVATING A SYSTEM WHERE NOTHING CAN HAPPEN
  If none of your ships can reach the system and you can't produce there,
  the game warns you first ("Back" / "Activate it anyway").

ACTION CARDS
  You draw 1 action card in each status phase (more with Politics and
  Neural Motivator); you can hold 7. When a card's moment comes - the start
  of a combat round, after you activate a system, when an agenda is
  revealed... - the game asks "Play an action card?" and lists only the
  cards you can play right then. B = don't play. You can play several cards
  at the same moment ("Play another action card?"); then the next player
  gets to respond.
  Cards marked "Action" are played as your whole turn: they appear in the
  "Choose your action" list, together with technologies that are used as an
  action (such as Sling Relay).
  Sabotage: when someone plays a card and you hold Sabotage, you're asked
  whether to cancel it.

AGENDA PHASE
  Once someone removes the custodians token from Mecatol Rex, every round
  ends with an agenda phase: two agendas are revealed and voted on.
  - Choose an outcome (For / Against, or who/what is elected) or Abstain,
    then choose which ready planets to exhaust: each gives votes equal to its
    influence. The speaker votes last and breaks ties.
  - Laws stay in play (MENU > Laws); directives happen once.
  - Riders (Imperial Rider, Trade Rider...) let you bet on an outcome
    instead of voting.
  - All planets are readied at the end of the agenda phase.

EXPLORATION AND RELICS (Prophecy of Kings)
  - When you take a planet nobody controlled, you explore it: a card from
    the deck matching its trait (cultural / hazardous / industrial) is
    resolved. Home planets and Mecatol Rex have no trait.
  - Some cards stay on the planet (attachments: more resources or influence,
    a technology specialty, or the Demilitarized Zone). They are shown as a
    small diamond on the planet; the side panel and the Planets screen list
    them. A DMZ planet (white diamond) can't receive units.
  - Relic fragments go to your play area. With 3 of one type (unknown
    fragments count as any type), choose "Relic fragments" in the action
    list to purge them and gain a relic. MENU > Your cards > Relics shows
    your relics, fragments and Enigmatic Devices; press A to read one.
  - Frontier tokens (small star) sit in empty systems. They are explored
    with Dark Energy Tap or the Exploration Probe card.
  - Gamma wormholes (pink) and the ion storm can appear from frontier cards.
    The ion storm flips (alpha <-> beta) after any move or retreat that
    goes through its wormhole.
  - The Wormhole Nexus waits beside the board (top left of the map). It
    only has its gamma wormhole until ships enter it or someone takes
    Mallice; then it opens its alpha and beta wormholes too.
  - Legendary planets (Hope's End, Primor, Mirage, Mallice) have an ability
    the game offers at the end of your turn.

PROMISSORY NOTES
  Everyone starts with their own Ceasefire, Trade Agreement, Political Secret
  and Support for the Throne (plus faction notes and the Alliance). They
  change hands in deals (Negotiations) and through game effects
  (Diplomatic Pressure); MENU > Your cards shows yours.

NEGOTIATIONS
  Open MENU: the left side shows the faction web. Each faction's emblem
  sits where its home system is around Mecatol Rex (MR), you at the bottom.
  The lines between two factions mean:
    grey    nothing agreed (brighter grey = neighbours, who can trade)
    cyan    a promise in force
    green   a deal in force (a promissory note handed over, or promises
            that came with a trade)
    red     clashed: they fought, or one broke a promise to the other
            (until their next trade)
  Choose "Negotiations" in the menu and move between factions with the
  D-pad. The right side shows that faction's trade goods and commodities,
  whether you can trade with them now, the promises between you and your
  past deals. Press A to make them an offer - possible when you are
  choosing your action, or before a vote. You can also pick "Negotiate"
  in your action list. Negotiating never uses up your action.

  Making an offer (or a counter-offer):
    You give / You ask for   trade goods, commodities (they turn into trade
                             goods for whoever receives them), one
                             promissory note, relic fragments. With the
                             Hacan also action cards (yours) and a Pride
                             of Kenara planet; with the Vuil'raith,
                             captured units back.
    Promises (up to 3)       who promises, what, where and for how many
                             rounds: no attacks, stay out of a system,
                             leave Mecatol Rex / the Wormhole Nexus, let
                             ships stay in a system, attack a system, pay
                             back a loan, use the agent for the other
                             player, vote for an outcome (before a vote).
    If they refuse           a threat: attack one of their systems.
  Up/Down choose a row, Left/Right (or A) change it, Y fills in a
  suggested deal, START sends it, B cancels. On the right you see the deal
  in words and how the other player is likely to take it. Systems named in
  the deal light up on the map (R2 to look).

  Answering an offer: Accept, Decline or Counter (Left/Right, then A).
  A counter-offer is final: the other side accepts or declines it.

  The rules (Living Rules Reference 94): on a player's turn in the action
  phase, that player can trade components with neighbours (the Hacan, and
  holders of their Trade Convoys, with anyone) - once per pair of players
  per turn. Before each vote, everyone can trade with everyone, once per
  pair. Each side gives at most one promissory note per trade. You can make
  at most 2 offers to each player per turn (declined ones count).
  Promises are never forced: the game only records whether they were kept.
  A broken promise turns the line red and is remembered by everyone
  ("Has broken 2 promises" on the panel). Loans fall due in the status
  phase: you're asked whether to pay back.
  Refusing to leave Mecatol Rex may make the asker attack you (anywhere
  from 0 to 100% likely; for the Wormhole Nexus, up to 25%).

PERSONALITIES
  Every computer player plays to win, but in its own way. Its archetype is
  secret until the end of round 3, then shown to everyone. Its evolution
  path, its two traits, its grudges and any betrayal it is planning stay
  secret all game.

  Archetypes
    Politician   balanced; strong in agendas; fair deals, and it keeps
                 asking until it gets one
    Peacekeeper  avoids first strikes, guards home, offers no-attack pacts
                 and ceasefires to its neighbours
    Warlord      attacks often, keeps big fleets (5 fleet tokens), demands
                 tribute from weaker players (only when it could win the
                 fight), takes only about 1 in 3 merely fair deals
    Betrayer     offers alliances and no-attack pacts - and is the most
                 likely to turn on its partners
    Swindler     careful in fights; sells promises for trade goods and
                 keeps only some of them
    Merchant     trades every chance it gets, lends trade goods, likes the
                 Trade card, leans on players now and then
    Opportunist  easy on players who leave it alone, hard on whoever
                 crowds its systems
    Rusher       races for Mecatol Rex and the Imperial card, holds them,
                 makes pacts with its neighbours
    Turtler      fills its own slice, guards it with big fleets and PDS,
                 rarely goes far - but hits back hard when attacked
    Chaotic      plays as a different archetype every round
  Each archetype values promissory notes, commodities and agents
  differently (the price lists you designed).

  Evolution paths (hidden; one per bot): Late Bloomer, Vengeful, Mirror,
  Kingslayer, Ambitious, Learner, Rising Diplomat, Border Watch, Scavenger,
  Three Acts - how the bot changes as the game goes on and as players
  treat it.
  Traits (hidden): one of 13 generic traits (Reckless, Holy Diver, Chess
  Master, Kneecapper, Tech Hoarder, Collector, Grudge Keeper, Bargain
  Hunter, Showboat, Cautious Admiral, Spoiler, Homebody, Gambler) and one
  of its faction's two traits. Traits only shift priorities - they never
  give a bot anything.

  Grudges and betrayals: each bot keeps a B-Factor toward each player. It
  grows when you refuse its deals, move next to its home or planets,
  take Mecatol Rex or a planet of its, enter the Wormhole Nexus, attack it
  or break a promise to it; fair deals, gifts and kept promises lower it.
  Every 8 of the bot's turns a d100 is rolled against its biggest grudge.
  If it comes up, the bot will soon take on a secret betrayal objective
  against that player: break a deal, pass their promissory note on, shake
  them down, hit their weakest system, park ships in their system, vote
  them down, refuse their offers for 2 rounds, take one of their planets,
  borrow and not repay, or turn another bot against them. When it's done
  (or after 3 rounds) the grudge goes back to its starting level.

  Attitude (after round 3): the faction web and the Negotiations panel
  show how each bot feels about you - Friendly, Wary or Hostile - judged
  from its grudge, your deals, your fights and your promises.
